Minpentai in this edition: the rules

One set of rules for the broadcast, the lessons, practice, free play, the computer ladder and one-on-one. Where v3 disagrees with this page, this page is the rule and v3 changes. Section 11 lists every change.

BOOK c4-b84the book says it; block cited INVENTEDours, for this edition OPENstill undecided; the default shown is used until then
The rules of this game are invented for this edition. In the book, the rule changes every match: "every game there's always some kind of new rule" (c4-b84). The cell rule recovered from the book's animated board (c4-b5, c4-b7) stays in Under the hood, unchanged.

1 · The board

Size
15 × 10 squares for lessons, practice, the ladder and one-on-one. Larger boards later (section 10).
INVENTED
Edges
Solid. A glider that would leave the board turns back. The board has edges and corners in the book.
BOOK c4-b113 · c7-b81
Starting layout
Each player starts with 2 towers in their home corner. The layout and the rocks are mirrored, so both sides start even.
INVENTED
Home corner
Setup happens near your own corner.
BOOK c4-b94

2 · The pieces

Tower
Your score, your sight, and where you may build. The book calls them symbols; here they are drawn as towers.
BOOK c4-b93SHAPE INVENTED
Glider
Moves one square per step in a straight line: up, down, left or right. Diagonals only when the match's new rule allows them. It never stops on its own.
BOOK c4-b84SPEED INVENTED
Square
Blocks a glider and sends it back. The first hit cracks it. The second hit breaks it, and the glider breaks too. Any glider cracks any square, yours included.
BOOK c2-b12 · c4-b9TWO HITS INVENTED
Rock
Fixed. Sends every glider straight back, every time. Never breaks. In the book, gliders hitting rocks can make more gliders; here they only bounce.
BOOK c4-b108OPEN: MORE GLIDERS?

3 · One step

INVENTED

Every glider moves at the same moment. Nothing depends on which glider the computer handles first. (In v3 gliders moved one at a time in list order, so the same board could play out two ways.)

  1. 1Each glider works out the square it is heading for.
  2. 2Gliders that meet break. Two gliders of different players break each other if they head for the same square, swap squares, or cross paths. Crossing includes two diagonal gliders passing through the same corner. A player's own gliders pass through each other.
  3. 3Each glider still flying then meets what is in its square: the edgeturns back on the side it would have left by; a diagonal hitting a corner turns fully back a rockturns straight back a whole squarethe square cracks; the glider turns straight back a cracked squareboth break an opponent's towerboth are destroyed your own towerturns straight back (in the book a stray glider can wreck its own side, c4-b109; kept out here) nothingmoves in
  4. 4Two gliders hitting one square in the same step are two hits. A whole square breaks, and the second glider breaks with it.
  5. 5Broken pieces leave nothing behind. (The book's boards fill with debris, c4-b140. Debris is drawn in the broadcast only, and gliders fly through it.)

4 · Turns to act

When
A setup turn at step 0, then every 12 steps. Between turns the match runs on its own.
BOOK c4-b110 · c14-b45OPEN: 12, 24 OR ~1,000
Points
8 points each turn. Glider 4, square 1, tower 4. Points not spent are lost.
INVENTED
Where
Any empty square within 3 squares (counting diagonals) of a tower of yours that stood when the turn began. The lit area. A tower placed this turn lights more squares from the next turn on.
BOOK c4-b110DISTANCE INVENTED
Changing your mind
Tap a piece you placed this turn to take it back and get its points back.
INVENTED
Who goes first
Nobody. Both players act at the same time and cannot see each other's choices. Everything placed appears when the run starts.
INVENTED
Time to act
Unlimited against the computer. 45 seconds in one-on-one; setup gets 90. When time runs out, what you placed stands.
BOOK c12-b149SECONDS INVENTED

5 · Sight

What you see
Every square within 3 of one of your towers, right now. The rest is dark.
BOOK c4-b933, NOT 30, INVENTED
Rocks
Shown everywhere, all match. The map is known.
INVENTED
Their towers
You see them where they started, because starts are mirrored. After that, a tower you cannot see is drawn as an outline marked "last seen, step 30". It may be gone.
INVENTED
The count
The strip shows your towers, and only the opponent towers you can see now: "AMBER 1 SEEN". The true count shows when the match ends.
INVENTED
Watching
The broadcast and anyone watching see the whole board. Players never do.
BOOK c14-b51

6 · How a match ends

Out
A player with no towers left is out. The last player with towers wins.
BOOK c4-b136–b138
Time
At step 96 the match stops. Most towers wins; equal is a draw. Games in the book have no fixed limit.
INVENTED
Resigning
Any player may resign at any time in practice, the ladder and one-on-one, from the ··· menu, after one confirmation. It counts as a loss. Computer players never resign, so you always see the end. Lessons have no resign; they have Reset.
BOOK c4-b141 · c10-b164
Leaving
In one-on-one, a player who disconnects has 60 seconds to come back. Steps pause meanwhile. After that it counts as resigning.
INVENTED

7 · The match's new rule

At the start of every practice, ladder and one-on-one match, one new rule is drawn at random and shown to both players at the same moment. Players learn it only when the match starts (c4-b84, c12-b133–b134). Lessons have none. In free play you pick.

Gliders may also fly diagonally.
Gliders cost 3 points.
You see only 2 squares around your towers.
Turns to act come every 8 steps.
ALL FOUR INVENTED · THE SAME FOUR AS V3

8 · Computer players

They play by every rule above. They see only what their own towers light, get the same 8 points, build towers, and fly diagonally when the rule allows it. Harder opponents plan better; they never know more. Results are the same for the same seed. INVENTED

In the book, bots are strong at the usual rules and adapt badly to a new one (c7-b13–b14). The ladder's upper rungs are the ones that use the new rule.

9 · Lessons and the broadcast

  • The broadcast replays a match played under these rules, re-recorded. Each drone is a glider. Every tower that falls is hit by one. The ending stays the same: Amber resigns.
  • Reset puts the lesson back where it began, goal included: the status clears and the big button offers the demo again.
  • Each demo uses its own move. Lesson 5's demo always fires right from the marked square, whatever arrow was last chosen, and the arrows show →.
  • Pressing the big button during a demo finishes the demo at once.
  • Slow motion at a hit lasts half a second, at 0.3× speed. It does not slow down under reduced motion.
  • When your turn to act is over in practice, a tap on the board says "Your turn to act is over. The next one is at step 24."

10 · Room to grow

Nothing above assumes two players or 15 × 10. Larger boards keep every rule, with more towers and the step limit raised. These are placeholders, not designed yet.
