Who he is. Zei Leimin is a young Minpentaiⓘ player from Pafogai Du, a city in Dzegoⓘ (c14-b31, c7-b61, c5-b69–b70). Minpentai is a strategy game that Dzego takes very seriously. Zei is quick and competitive, and he plays so cleverly that Den, who recruits him into defense, says he nearly reinvented parts of cryptography in a single game (c9-b66, c9-b75).

At the start. In the middle of a lecture, an Arcticⓘ bomb tears through his classroom. A stranger had moved his class to another room minutes before, which saved his life, but a stone breaks his left arm (c2-b98–b104, c5-b72, c9-b75). He's playing for prize money to buy his mom a new compute boxⓘ (c4-b73). His rival Bai has beaten him three times out of three, yet in a big match he hands her the deciding turn: "your chance to shine" (c4-b72, c4-b129). He wins the money (c7-b120, c7-b138), takes on secret defense work he can't tell even his best friend Fin about (c9-b73, c10-b206), and reaches the top four of the qualifiers (c14-b31).

Later in the book (spoilers). Mu tells him what Minpentai is really for: "It's military training." (c10-b191–b194). When the war comes, Zei directs drone tactics against the Arctics (c29-b110, c30-b2). He loses Den (c29-b108, c29-b115). Deluin, now secretly a commander on the Arctic side, sends him a way through and access to the Arctic drones' simulator (c29-b114, c30-b4), and his side wins back Northgladeⓘ (c30-b68–b72). The championship final is cancelled and Zei is given the title (c31-b26). By the end, he argues that "AI should be a player, not the game." (c32-b42–b47)

What drives him. Winning, and what it buys his mom. After the bomb, something bigger: a stranger saved his life, and he wonders if it's his turn to do the same for someone else (c9-b76).
