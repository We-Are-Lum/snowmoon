Chapters 17, 18 and 19 contain no conflict blocks in either pass. Chapters 2 and 5 contain 0–1%. Explanation runs highest in c25 (60%), c9 (54%), c22 (52%) and c2 (47%). Conflict runs highest in c7 (55%), c21 (51%), c28 (48%), c8 (47%) and c14 (47%).

4. **Offscreen.** Both invasions, of Redshire (c15-b8) and of Northglade (c20-b8), arrive as news text. Den's death arrives as a screen report (c29-b108) and is next mentioned in one line about his funeral (c31-b93). Lektor's takeover of the military is relayed by Gladias (c28-b93–c28-b96). 5. **Costly choices.** Gladias makes 24 on the page. Seila makes 9 on the page and 4 reported. Bai makes 7 on the page and 1 reported. Deluin makes 2 on the page, both in a Minpentai match and its aftermath (c14), and 7 reported. Lily makes 0 on the page and 6 reported.
6. **Waiting.** The longest wait for an answer is 15 chapters: who killed Bai's parents (c9-b14, answered at c24-b48). The question of who is behind Delwart is answered in three steps over 10–15 chapters; the first answer (Silverchat, c16-b13) is false and is overturned at c20-b179 and c21-b71. Eleven threads are never answered, among them the purple letter "For Zei's eyes only" (c7-b143), which is never opened on the page, and how Delwart tracked Gladias down (c3-b86).

## How this was produced

- **Reading.** Eight readers each read four chapters in full against a shared brief. Every claim is tied to a block ID and a verbatim fragment of 15 words or fewer, and a script confirmed that each of the 753 fragments appears in the block it cites. I then traced the questions across chapters and checked the long-range answers and unanswered threads myself, with searches across all 32 chapters.
- **Limits.** "Wants", "obstacle" and "cost" are readings of the text, not counts. Where a want is implied rather than stated, the cited block is where the inference rests. POV boundaries follow datelines and section breaks; brief dips into another character's head are noted in the POV line, not split out.

## c. Explanation of a system versus characters in conflict

### Method

- **Unit.** Every block of kind `paragraph`, `quote` or `screen`: 4,105 blocks. Headings, datelines, section breaks and figures are excluded.
- **Measure.** The share of each chapter's words, counted after stripping HTML markup (103,196 words in total). Block-count shares run in the same direction and are not shown.
- **Labels.** Each block gets exactly one:
  - **SYS:** the block's main job is to tell the reader how something works or is: technology, governance, economics, law, history, institutions, AI, customs, ideology or geography. The explanation is cooperative: narration, a lecture, a document or screen, or dialogue where the listener accepts.
  - **CONF:** active opposition between characters present in the scene, or between a character and a group or institution acting in the scene, with something at stake for at least one of them. This covers arguments (including arguments about systems, when one side pushes against the other and the outcome matters in the scene), refusals, threats, negotiation, deception of someone present, combat, and a player acting against an opponent in a match. Reaction beats inside such an exchange count.
  - **OTHER:** everything else, such as description, travel, routine action, interiority with no one opposing, small talk, cooperative planning, and conflict that is only recounted.
- **Two passes.** Two independent sets of readers labelled every block. Pass 1 used the first rubric. Spot-checks showed pass 1 applied the CONF line unevenly. One group labelled the Sylka–Ephelion debate (c8) as CONF, while another labelled the parallel argument with Evelor (c27-b77–c27-b95) as SYS and OTHER. Pass 2 re-labelled all blocks with the tie-breaks above made explicit, using a different chapter grouping. **Pass 2 is the primary figure.**
- **Agreement.** The passes agree on 87.1% of blocks. Of the 530 disagreements, 326 are OTHER in pass 1 and CONF in pass 2, so pass 2 reads more blocks as conflict. Chapters where agreement falls below 80% (c3, c4, c7, c21, c26, c28, c32) have the least stable figures.
