# Snowmoon: Chapter Notes

**Coverage:** All 32 chapters, each read in full.

**What this is:** A working reading aid in plain summary form. It is not quote-verified and it is not the analysis pipeline from the build brief. Check any detail against the text before building on it. It contains full spoilers.

**Source:** *Snowmoon* by Vitalik Buterin, https://vitalik.eth.limo/snowmoon/ (GPL v3).

## The story in brief

In a world of ninety million people, the expansionist Arctic Empire is quietly hollowing out its neighbors. Gladias, a new rule-setter in Veridia's anonymous Order of Steering, is manipulated by an Arctic agent. Zei, a teenage game champion in suppressed Dzego, is recruited into its hidden defense society. After the Arctics seize Redshire and then part of Veridia itself, the two threads join. Gladias's wife Seila exposes the agent, Veridia's command is reformed from within, and a joint force retakes the north by tricking the Arctic drones with printed patterns, helped by Zei's friend Deluin acting as an insider. The book ends on a debate about how a free society can cooperate like water and defend like ice.

The story opens on Snowmoon 3, 3724, and the decisive battle falls on Snowmoon 29 and 30, 3725.

## Structure

- **Part 1, chapters 1 to 8: two worlds.** Gladias is pressured by a stranger, Delwart, into becoming a Keeper and joins the Order by one vote. Zei survives a bombing and wins his city's Minpentai title. The threads touch once, in chapter 5, when Zei and Bai meet Seila in Freetown.
- **Part 2, chapters 9 to 16: recruitment and the first blow.** Zei is recruited by the Kungaupei and learns Minpentai is disguised military training. Gladias finds Delwart is running an organized campaign. In chapter 15 the Arctics annex Redshire and trap Zei's friend Deluin. Delwart gives a false confession.
- **Part 3, chapters 17 to 24: convergence and loss.** Gladias goes to Dzego. The Arctics take Northglade with two of the children inside. Seila proves Delwart is an Arctic agent. Lily, won over by Arctic ideas, runs away. Gladias proposes that Dzego spy on Veridia to find the infiltration.
- **Part 4, chapters 25 to 32: the counter-move.** The team rescues Redshire's captive heir, uses a mass poll to show what Veridians want, and gets a trusted general, Lektor, put in command. The battle for Northshore is nearly lost, Den is killed, and Deluin's inside help turns it. The family is reunited and the closing chapter looks ahead.

Who each chapter follows: 1 Gladias, 2 Zei, 3 Gladias, 4 Zei, 5 Zei and Seila, 6 Gladias, 7 Zei, 8 Gladias, 9 Zei with Bai and Fin, 10 all three places, 11 Gladias, 12 Zei, 13 Gladias, 14 Zei, 15 all three places, 16 Gladias, 17 Gladias, 18 Gladias arriving in Dzego, 19 Gladias and Zei, 20 Seila, 21 Seila, 22 Jahn and Zei, 23 Gladias in Dzego, 24 Gladias in Dzego, 25 Gladias and Zei, 26 everyone in Meldan, 27 Gladias, Seila, and Zei, 28 the Dzego team then the family, 29 Zei, 30 Zei, 31 Zei then the family, 32 Gladias, Seila, Zei, and Verdow.

Months in order of appearance: Snowmoon, Rainmoon, Windmoon, Bloomtime, Grasstime, Harvestmoon, Firemoon, Fruitmoon, Grapetime, Mistime, Frostime (all 3724), then Snowmoon and Rainmoon of 3725.

## Ideas the book keeps returning to

- **Privacy protects institutions.** The anonymous, randomly selected bodies (the Order, the Courts, Graph Funding) resist capture. The transparent ones (Parliament, the military, public funding rounds) are bought, blackmailed, or gamed.
- **Entropy and reversibility.** From the first physics lesson to hash functions, the pile game, and the final battle, the same idea recurs: information you lack, moves you can undo, and states you cannot escape.
- **Coordination without a head.** Dzego's motto, its language reforms, and its security rollout all show a society changing together with no leader.
- **Winning by hiding your thinking,** and by knowing more at the moment you move.
- **Open technology as defense.** Open designs keep any one power from being the only one protected.
- **Earned love versus unconditional love.** Ephelion's creed, Bai's childhood, and Lily's arc. The book lands on "no single formula."
- **Water and ice.** The closing image: flow freely in peace, align and harden under threat.

## World notes

- **Veridia.** Green, wooded, stone-built, calm. Few things are banned; behavior is steered by taxes and subsidies. Capital: Meldan, with its forested Kalimar district. Its people are slow to take the Arctic threat seriously.
- **Order of Steering.** Twenty-one Keepers per tax rubric, in three anonymous groups of seven, addressed by number, write the rubrics. Sentinels audit businesses in three groups of three. Acolytes are trainees scored on predicting Sentinel judgments. Heralds are randomly retired members who explain the system in public. Exposing a member's task earns a bounty. Votes use keys registered in person so no one can prove or sell a vote.
- **Courts.** Five randomly selected anonymous judges per case, a fresh panel on each appeal, and penalties for bad-faith filings.
- **Privacy Robe.** Dark purple, hooded, full body, with face cover, matching shoes, and insoles that disguise height and gait.
- **Devices.** Watch, hand device, neck band for silent speech, earpiece, a local AI called Emerald. Green circles to tap for entry and payment. Constant zero-knowledge checks on drones and devices. Money is zipcoins, held in wallets with social recovery.
- **Funding terms.** Quadratic Funding (public, and repeatedly gamed), land tax with components such as aesthetics and indoor air quality, and Graph Funding, where money flows down a graph of topics, each split is set by a random committee, and winners above a quality cutoff are drawn by lot.
- **Helisport.** A sport from Dzego played in caged personal copters. Redballs are caught, greenballs splat opponents with paint, blueballs knock down pins. The three counts multiply into the score, which rewards balance. Lily uses this to defy her Arctic teacher.
- **Dzego.** Low-rise, dense, electronic, playful cartoon aesthetics, underground rooms lined with anti-transmission foil, mountains refurbished as pyramids. Cities: Pafogai Du and the capital Sadzu Du. Motto: grow roots, no head.
- **Dzegoban and the Bansunpei.** An engineered language of 256 roots, managed by a body whose members are called priests. Its real purpose is to train the population to change behavior together. Dzego also gave the world its decimal clock.
- **Kungaupei.** Dzego's secret defense society. Minpentai is its disguised training and recruiting ground.
- **Minpentai.** A cellular-automaton style game: players place cells that form gliders, walls, factories, and spaceships. You see only near copies of your own symbol. Every match adds a secret rule.
- **Arctic Empire.** The antagonist power, ruled by an Emperor from the ice-crystal capital Glasscrown. Growth and hierarchy above all, real and impressive technology kept strictly to itself, total surveillance. It works through open propaganda (Lord Ephelion), covert agents (Delwart), bribery and blackmail, and lightning annexations.
- **United Cities.** A federation whose cities elect shares of each other's parliaments. Includes Freetown, Redshire, Devanvil, Inglewore, Hapton, Arturia, and the cluster Ondak, Iptak, Mantak, Belpaki. Freetown has a minimal government, and its megacorps fund its defense.
- **Greater Plum Harbor (GPH).** Walled corporate enclaves, grey and purple, uniform and futuristic, in Meldan, Freetown, and Northglade. The Arctics leave the Northglade enclave alone. The book never explains why.
- **Platforms.** Silverchat (open, verifiable, founded by Evelor; Delwart's claim to work for it was a lie) and Bluewhale (closed, hostile to open platforms).
- **Time.** 100 ticks to a minute, 100 minutes to a longhour, 10 longhours to a day.
- **Security habits in Dzego.** Sealed black cars with camera-fed windows, cabins shuffled between cars in tunnels, goods delivered through a physical mixnet of sealed boxes, cameras anyone may take down and inspect, data hidden inside ordinary-looking streams.

## What the ending leaves open

- Redshire stays under Arctic rule. An exchange of people and a "new Redshire" are proposed, not settled.
- The Arctic Emperor is expected to try again within five to twenty years.
- Delwart is never caught, and the camera bug in the Keeper booth is never traced.
- Why the Arctics spared the Greater Plum Harbor.
- How Veridia's government should change. The last chapter debates it and does not decide.
- Bai's year in Thaldur, Zei's stay in Veridia, and the fate of Deluin's parents.

## Notes for adaptation

- **Set pieces:** five Minpentai matches (chapters 4, 7, 12, 14, plus a friendly in 10), two Helisport sequences (11 and 16), two remote drone operations shown as tactical maps (22 and 26), and the two-chapter battle (29 and 30). All need a designed, consistent visual language rendered in code.
- **The climax is fought from chairs.** Zei watches maps and feeds. The strongest images are at the edges: the snow hill at sunrise, the drones trailing fiber, soldiers pasting paper at dawn, the DOG sheets.
- **Both invasions happen offscreen,** as news bulletins and messages. An adaptation has to choose whether to keep that restraint.
- **Three leads, not one.** Gladias, Zei, and Seila each carry chapters. Bai, Mu, and Deluin are close behind.
- **The book is bookended.** Chapter 32 retraces chapter 1's walk through Kalimar, with the same sky bridge, tower apartments, aeroplane building, and a Hydrafill ad. The camera being unscrewed in Meldan echoes the one in Dzego in chapter 4. Illustrators should design chapter 1's locations knowing they return.
- **Lily's arc is planted early and quietly:** silent at dinners, a hidden game, staring at the broadcast in chapter 13, rising scores. Early chapters should be drawn with that in mind, without the reader app spoiling it. The same goes for Delwart, whose identity flips twice.
- **Screens are everywhere:** vote sliders, tax and rubric tables, ballots, messages, receipts, diagrams, scoreboards, funding graphs, pie charts, maps.
- **Much of the Gladias thread is walking, observing, and debating in booths.** It will need captions and strong environments. Keepers and judges are robed, which makes group scenes hard to read visually.
- **Faces:** robed characters are hidden, which helps consistency. Home scenes, the Delwart booth, the hearing, and nearly all of Dzego carry the faces.
- **Language:** Dzegoban appears constantly, including long untranslated blocks in chapters 23 and 26 that a character translates line by line. Lettering needs a firm convention.
- **Seasons matter.** Green Meldan for most of the book, snow for the battle and Northglade.

## Possible continuity slips in the source

These are things to check, not confirmed errors. Each is also noted under its chapter.

- Chapter 15: a section headed Pafogai Du reads as taking place in Sadzu Du.
- Chapters 23 and 24: dated five days apart, though 24 opens the morning after 23 ends.
- Chapter 26: the rescue target is the presidential residence, but one line says parliament building; a judge's name is spelled two ways; "planes" where cars seem meant.
- Chapter 28: Bai refers to Zei speaking with Lektor, but Den did the talking.
- Chapters 17 and 31: Northglade is "over two hundred kilometers inland" in one and on a small island in the other.

## Chapter 1
**Setting:** Meldan, Veridia. 3724 Snowmoon 3. **Thread:** Gladias.

Gladias, an Acolyte training for Veridia's Order of Steering, walks through the wooded Kalimar district to a metal concert. On the way he is picked by sortition to cast an aesthetics vote on an apartment building, which introduces the land tax and quadratic voting. At the concert his real task is to rate the band Dreadknot on a social-impact tax rubric. He picks the neutral Tier 3, risking his prediction score. A bystander outs him as an Order member. A stranger in a robe runs as a decoy, Gladias snatches a pursuer's device, wipes the footage, and escapes by autobus. At home with Seila and four children (Zven and Lily are theirs; Febric and Hreda belong to their co-family, Vil and Daia, who will soon take all four for six months), he receives an anonymous message from someone who saw him and wants a favor.

**People:** Gladias, Seila, Zven, Lily, Febric, Hreda (mentioned), Emerald (his local AI), the burly man. **Places:** Kalimar street, sky bridge, concert field, autobus, home. **Visual keys:** dark purple Privacy Robe, hand device and watch, toy drone, Hydrafill ads, vote and tax screens.

## Chapter 2
**Setting:** Pafogai Du, Dzego. 3724 Snowmoon 5. **Thread:** Zei.

Teenage friends Fin and Zei walk down Hun Min street, a low-rise district of electronics shops and tiny factories, to a secret Decentralized University (DU) physics class whose room is revealed by a cryptographic broadcast minutes before it starts. They are redirected to a different room at the last moment. The instructor teaches entropy as information you do not know, and Zei gives the key argument from time-reversible physics. An explosion in the room they were originally assigned brings down part of the wall and breaks Zei's arm. Afterward the instructor explains the stakes: the Arctic Empire keeps Dzego's industry suppressed, and Dzego answers with "grow roots, no head", meaning distributed manufacturing and anonymous, leaderless coordination.

**People:** Zei, Fin, the instructor. **Places:** Hun Min street, underground foil-lined classroom with cartoon animal murals (pig with calculator, hamster with vial). **Visual keys:** blinking shop signs, anti-transmission foil, lecture slides of gas jars, smoke and rubble.

## Chapter 3
**Setting:** Meldan, Veridia. 3724 Snowmoon 8. **Thread:** Gladias.

In a pedestrian tunnel Gladias reads an Arctic Empire propaganda poster signed by Lord Ephelion, the Arctic consul, arguing that Veridia is comfortable but weak. His friend Mov, an established Order member, meets him, gives him a fresh robe, and will shadow him. Mov describes a fight over taxing the platform Bluewhale for closing its API, and a Keeper whose exposure cost her husband his job. Seila sends photos from Freetown. Gladias enters Greater Plum Harbor (GPH), a walled corporate enclave with uniform white, grey, and purple futuristic buildings that many Veridians resent. In a curtained booth he meets his anonymous contact, Delwart, who calls Steering a tyranny of prejudice and asks him to become a Keeper, not a Sentinel, and vote to delete every rubric. Delwart mixes an offer of protection with a veiled threat. As he leaves, a robed figure (Mov) follows him.

**People:** Gladias, Mov, Delwart, Seila (by message), Lord Ephelion (by poster). **Places:** tunnel, street with construction barriers, GPH wall and interior, hidden courtyard booth. **Visual keys:** taped poster, Hydrafill "hydrogen hydroxide" ad, GPH castle-like wall, serving robot with smiley face, air and device readout on the watch.

## Chapter 4
**Setting:** Pafogai Du, Dzego. 3724 Snowmoon 24. **Thread:** Zei.

At a courtyard cafe Zei studies a Minpentai replay while Fin explains that the language body, the Bansunpei, is redesigning written Dzegoban to be visual and two-dimensional. Zei, his arm healed, climbs to the mountain refurbished as a half-kilometer pyramid for the Minpentai quarter-finals. His rival Bai taunts him. Players surrender their devices and play in a dark arena before more than a thousand spectators. Zei is shocked to be teamed with Bai against Pan and Dza. The two barely speak, lose ground, and then Zei engineers a spaceship that assembles Bai's symbol inside Pan's base, handing her the decisive move. They win. Their coach Mu reveals the hidden rule change: teams were not random, and the match was a test of whether rivals would cooperate. Bai apologizes.

**People:** Zei Leimin, Fin, Bai Jahen, Mu, Pan Munbau, Dza Dzingo. **Places:** courtyard cafe, the pyramid mountain and its stone corridors, the arena. **Visual keys:** teenager removing a street camera, picture-based robot menu, Bansunpei logo (two arms clasping hands) versus the arena symbol (two arms with clashing fists), the Minpentai board with gliders, walls, and symbols.

## Chapter 5
**Setting:** Freetown, United Cities. 3724 Rainmoon 11. **Thread:** Zei, crossing with Seila.

Zei, his mother Min, and Bai arrive in Freetown on a trip Bai offered after their match (the Redshire flights were cancelled because of Arctic drones). Both won their semi-finals, so the two will face each other in the final, and Zei reasons a shared break costs neither an edge. Bai admits she wanted his company. At the spiral-floored Freetown Economics Institute, a lecture explains United Cities co-governance, where paired cities elect a share of each other's parliament. Zei and Bai sit down with two strangers: Seila, the Veridian blogger, and her local friend Jahn. Zei casually mentions the classroom bombing and describes Dzego's auditable, cryptographic camera system. Later Gladias calls Seila and says he may become a Keeper. Seila, moved by the Dzego teenagers, urges him to do it and to shape rubrics that help Dzego.

**People:** Zei, Min, Bai, Seila, Jahn, Gladias (by call). **Places:** air harbor, taxi through rich and run-down districts, hotel, the Institute and its restaurant. **Visual keys:** passenger copters, a GPH wall in the distance, check-in pole with green circle, co-governance diagram of fifteen cities.

## Chapter 6
**Setting:** Meldan, Veridia. 3724 Rainmoon 12 and 13. **Thread:** Gladias.

In his foil-lined basement, Gladias tells Mov he will apply to be a Keeper. Mov guesses Delwart is behind it and reports what his tailing found: Delwart moves between GPH tea shops holding meeting after meeting, and is recognizable only because he never looks around. They agree to play along and watch. Gladias studies how Keeper panels work (three groups of seven) and the rubrics up for review. The next day he and Mov attend a citizens' assembly on public spaces run by Alagael, a Herald, where a teenager named Pelae asks how it all works and a flu detection triggers calm, practiced precautions. Gladias runs into Febric at a restaurant, then covers a declined payment with a reputation-backed anonymous loan. He climbs the long mountain staircase to a tower where ten robed Order members receive his decision: Keeper.

**People:** Gladias, Mov, Alagael, Pelae, Febric, the Order panel. **Places:** basement safe room, his residential street, community center, restaurant, mountain staircase and open-topped stone tower. **Visual keys:** rain, Keeper selection diagram, rubric table, violet far-UVC ceiling lights, masks and neck bands, loan and payment screens, green-lit tower.

## Chapter 7
**Setting:** Pafogai Du, Dzego. 3724 Rainmoon 23 and 24. **Thread:** Zei.

The day before the city final, Zei tells Fin that practice against bots has stopped helping, because everything turns on the surprise rule. Fin skips his language-body training to build him a better practice setup. Walking up the pyramid with Bai the next day, they feel a blast and see smoke: Dzego's largest chip plant, a centralized government facility, has been hit. In the final, each player gets a personal AI, but both sides can read the other AI's reasoning. Zei attacks a gap he sees in Bai's AI transcript and walks into a trap she built by hand. Behind on symbols, he has his AI prepare sixteen possible strategies in one hidden factory, then blocks its trigger and substitutes his own so the choice never appears in the transcript. The surprise breaks Bai's lines and she resigns. Mu explains the lesson: thinking well is not enough, you also have to hide your thinking, and she compares this to Veridia's anonymous Order versus its transparent Parliament. Zei's prize bag holds something Bai's does not: a dark purple letter marked for him alone.

**People:** Zei, Fin, Bai, Mu. **Places:** courtyard cafe, the climb, the arena, the waiting room. **Visual keys:** rating slider with faces on the robot, distant smoke plume over the city, split-screen AI transcripts, the board flipping from 17 to 22 down to a 12 to 6 win, the purple letter.

## Chapter 8
**Setting:** Meldan, Veridia. 3724 Rainmoon 26. **Thread:** Gladias.

Seila sends photos from Iptak. On the autobus, passengers watch a live debate between Professor Sylka, president of Veridia National University, and Lord Ephelion. Sylka argues that output alone is a poor measure of a society; Ephelion argues that softer goods only move in cycles while growth compounds. Gladias finds the argument irritatingly well made. At the circular Parliament he faces a twenty-senator selection hearing. Senator Papli attacks him as out of touch, Senator Ancus defends him, and a timer cuts off his best answers. The vote ties ten to ten and the Chairman breaks it in his favor. He goes straight to Vil's house, where Lily is crying over a failed civics test, and tries to comfort her. That evening Mov reports he has been randomly selected to become a Herald, so he loses formal power but will have more time to help. Gladias tells Seila he is in.

**People:** Gladias, Seila (by message and call), Sylka, Ephelion, Senators Verdow, Papli, and Ancus, the Chairman, Vil, Lily, Mov. **Places:** autobus stop and autobus, the Parliament chamber, Vil's house. **Visual keys:** red and brown brick-and-wood Iptak photos, debate livestream on a stranger's screen, ring of senators with green-lit seats, countdown timer, vote tally screen.

## Chapter 9
**Setting:** Pafogai Du, Dzego. 3724 Rainmoon 28. **Thread:** Zei, with Bai and Fin.

Bai, in tears, tells Fin what neither boy ever asked: her parents were killed in a lab explosion when she was small, an aunt gave her a room and little else, and winning was all she had. Fin distracts her with the history of Dzegoban, the engineered language, and the decimal clock Dzego gave the world. Meanwhile the purple letter leads Zei deep into the pyramid to Den, a High Council member of the Kungaupei, a secret defense society. Den says Zei's sixteen-strategy factory was a crude reinvention of a core idea in cryptography, hiding a program inside a general one, and recruits him: keep playing through the nationals, but switch his studies to cryptography. A sealed black car takes them to an underground chip plant, where Den shows how chips are shipped through a physical mixnet so inspectors and buyers draw from the same pool, and how a scanning microscope checks chips against their design. Zei agrees, and realizes he cannot tell his mother.

**People:** Bai, Fin, Zei, Den. **Places:** courtyard cafe, the deep pyramid corridors and Den's long room, the black car, the underground fab and inspection room. **Visual keys:** Bai crying at a cafe table, the Dzegoban booklet, Den's shadowy priest cloak (not a Veridian robe), car door sealed to building door, conveyor of white tamper-proof boxes with barcodes, mixnet diagram, large microscope.

## Chapter 10
**Setting:** Freetown, then Meldan, then Pafogai Du. 3724 Windmoon 24. **Thread:** all three places.

In Freetown, Jahn learns from Utaku, a doctoral student from Belpaki, that prices are up because the megacorps are funding defense against rising Arctic drone activity, and are coercing holdouts such as the carmaker Mogna. In Meldan, Gladias attends his first two Keeper groups, where members go by numbers. In the first he argues that the cultural values rubric makes art bland and that Dreadknot, now pushed to Tier 4, does not promote violence; the group agrees to reread the research and survey public sentiment. Before the second, a Keeper called Eighteen sweeps the booth and finds a needle-sized camera bug in the table. That group debates exempting military hardware from the openness rubric. Zero argues for staying out of arms races and doubts the Arctic threat, echoing Delwart; Eighteen argues open designs are Dzego's lifeline. Gladias listens. In Dzego, Bai beats Zei in a friendly game and will be his sparring partner. Den and Mu then tell him the truth: Minpentai is disguised military training, built to raise defenders.

**People:** Jahn, Utaku, Gladias, Seila, Keepers known as Twenty, One, Eleven, Eighteen, Nine, Zero, Zei, Bai, Den, Mu. **Places:** the Institute restaurant, a courtyard of heavy soundproof booths with white-noise hum, a Dzego courtyard, Den's long room. **Visual keys:** robed Keepers on benches reading hand devices, Emerald's live thinking window, a drone sweeping a booth, the needle bug held up and snapped, Mu leaning forward for the reveal.

## Chapter 11
**Setting:** Meldan, Veridia. 3724 Bloomtime 15. **Thread:** Gladias and family.

At a school field, both families watch Zven's first real Helisport game: children strapped into caged personal copters chase flying redballs. Vil explains his class-action suit: his employer Jump was quietly bought by the owners of the hedge fund Divergent, which took the other side of Jump's risky bet so that bankruptcy would wipe out staff bonuses and severance. Zven ignores the pack, hunts for the forgotten fourth ball, finds it on the ground with broken rotors, and scores the winning point. Seila nudges Gladias to do something for schools. In a third Keeper group, on indoor air quality, members argue over raising the tax ceiling, the risk of an engineered pandemic, privacy risks of wastewater scanning, and a quadratic funding round that Bluewhale secretly gamed to starve open platforms. Walking home, Gladias and Seila notice that Freetown's corporations take the Arctic threat more seriously than Veridia does, and that newer Keepers distrust public institutions altogether.

**People:** Gladias, Seila, Vil, Daia, Zven, the instructor, other children (Gale, Dela, Dommus, and more), unnamed Keepers. **Places:** grass field with a truck of copters, Keeper booth, the walk home, their house. **Visual keys:** yellow versus purple team shirts, caged rotor chairs, red ball drones, Zven running the broken ball to the instructor, air readout on the watch, Hydrafill "heavy water" ad with mascot shielding a child.

## Chapter 12
**Setting:** Sadzu Du, Dzego's capital. 3724 Grasstime 22 and 25. **Thread:** Zei.

Zei arrives by train for the national championship: thirty-two players, six qualifying rounds, top four advance. Priests in robes meet him, and sealed black cars carry contestants between hotel and venue. On the drive he passes the site of another blast, at a biology lab. At orientation he meets Deluin, a friendly foreign contestant from Redshire, one of four admitted this year. Bai calls: Den's people have sent her to the capital to study every contestant's play. In his first match, against local player Bo Zume, the surprise rule is a hexagonal grid, so every known pattern is useless and players get twenty minutes to rediscover the game. Zei recalls his cryptography textbook on reversible building blocks, works out how to run the hex rules backward, and uses that to design gliders that assemble his symbol on impact. He finds Bo's base and wins quickly.

**People:** Zei, Su (organizer), Taskmaster Jin, Deluin, Bai (by call), Bo Zume. **Places:** train and station, capital streets with health shops and a park with smaller pyramids, hotel, the grand arena holding thousands. **Visual keys:** priests in robes on a platform, taped-off blast site, textbook hash diagram, hex-grid board ("a field of snowflakes"), Zei hammering a button sequence to step time backward.

## Chapter 13
**Setting:** Meldan, Veridia. 3724 Harvestmoon 8 and 9. **Thread:** Gladias.

Gladias reports to Delwart that nearly half the first-year Keepers are already arguing for fewer rubrics, and Delwart tells him to talk colleagues out of worrying about the Arctics. Gladias hides his real doubts. Mov then calls: he overheard Delwart coaching a second Keeper from a different discussion group on the same hardware openness rubric, which defeats the purpose of keeping groups independent. This is an organized campaign. At a family dinner with all four children home, Hreda's high math score silences Lily. The restaurant screen is hijacked by an Ephelion broadcast arguing that people are not equal and that unconditional love is poison; Seila rushes to unplug it while Lily stares, then retreats into her game. The next day, in the hardware openness group, Zero proposes stripping military, surveillance, and counter-surveillance products of favorable tax tiers. Now suspicious, Gladias stalls with a test: wait two months, and let whether the Arctics attack Redshire decide the vote.

**People:** Gladias, Delwart, Mov, Seila, Zven, Lily, Febric, Hreda, Keepers Thirteen, Zero, Eighteen, One. **Places:** GPH curtained booth, the autobus, a family restaurant, the soundproof Keeper booth. **Visual keys:** Ephelion's face and manifesto filling a restaurant screen, Seila reaching to unplug it, Lily tapping at her hand device, robed Keepers hard to tell apart.

## Chapter 14
