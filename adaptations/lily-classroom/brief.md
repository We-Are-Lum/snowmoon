# Seed brief: Lily in the Arctic classroom

> **Full spoilers** for chapters 1–31.

**The adaptation:** Lily contradicts her Arctic civics instructor and is put in detention. The book never shows this scene. Zven reports it to Gladias and Seila, about Lily, in a hotel room (c28-b59–c28-b68). The adaptation draws on:
- the Helisport scoring argument (c16-b62–c16-b75), which is the substance of her objection;
- Lord Ephelion's broadcast that she stared at in chapter 13 (c13-b61–c13-b69);
- her leaving home in chapter 23 (c23-b79–c23-b99).

**Source:** the committed chapter text in `content/snowmoon/text/`. Block IDs are `c<chapter>-b<idx>`.

**Dates:**
- **Zven's report:** 3724 Frostime 15 (dateline c28-b1). The class itself is undated. Lily was talking to Zven "the whole time" (c28-b64), so the report comes after the class.
- **The broadcast:** 3724 Harvestmoon 8 (c13-b1).
- **Her leaving:** reported by Seila in a call on the night of 3724 Mistime 22 (c23-b1, c23-b74).

### The scoring argument

The book lays out the scoring argument three times:
- Febric's history of the sport (c16-b68–c16-b73).
- The scoreboards (c16-b62, c16-b66). Each combined score is the product of the three counts: 6 × 9 × 31 = 1,674; 11 × 7 × 23 = 1,771; 6 × 10 × 31 = 1,860. The text never states the formula; it says the score was made "multiplicative" (c16-b70).
- Lily's version, through Zven (c28-b64). She says that with added points the best strategy is almost always to focus on one ball type, and that multiplying makes balance pay.

## 2. What the text gives and what an adapter must invent

### Given

- The argument on both sides, nearly word for word, though only in Zven's retelling (c28-b63–c28-b66).
- That Lily stood up (c28-b64).
- That she was not alone. Zven was on her neck band "the whole time", Febric joined, and "We went through the history of Helisport" (c28-b64).
- The instructor's gesture ("threw up his hands") and his claim (c28-b65).
- The punishment (c28-b67) and her conclusion (c28-b68).
- The ideas she is answering:
  - The instructor's "focusing on only one thing" (c28-b65) restates Ephelion's case for the one vector that compounds (c8-b35).
  - Ephelion's broadcast pre-empts the multi-dimensional reply: "Do not think that the idea that there are multiple dimensions to goodness and to success saves you from this" (c13-b64).
- What she believed when she left: success-conditioned love as motivation (c23-b88). This echoes the broadcast's "give you love when you win, and a cold shoulder when you lose" (c13-b64).

### To invent

- **Lily's appearance.** The text gives none. Her only physical descriptions are posture and action: eyes to the ground (c1-b117), turning away (c1-b121, c8-b114), crying (c8-b110), staring (c13-b66), running into a room (c21-b18) and a hug (c31-b77). Her age is never stated.
- **The room, the building, the other students, the language of instruction, and what Arctic "civics" covers.**
- **The instructor's name, face, age and voice.** The text gives only "he" (c28-b65).
- **How the neck band conversation works in a classroom without being caught,** and whether the instructor notices.
- **What detention is in an Arctic boarding school, and how long it lasts.**
- **How Lily got from Meldan to the school.** The text gives only the facts in c26-b62–c26-b63 and the open travel in c26-b66.
- **What the game on her hand device is** (c16-b80). Whether it connects to the Arctic chat group (c23-b84) is not said.

## 5. Open creative questions

1. **Whose voice tells it?** Options include Lily in the first person, the book's own frame of Zven relaying it in the hotel room (c28-b59–c28-b68), or both. And how much of the argument is hers, and how much is fed live by Zven and Febric through the neck band (c28-b64)?
2. **Does she know whom she is answering?** The instructor's line (c28-b65) is Ephelion's argument (c8-b35, c13-b64). The broadcast already dismissed the "multiple dimensions" reply that she makes. Does she know she is also answering what she herself told Seila on the night she left (c23-b88)?
3. **What does the room look like, and who is in it?** The text gives nothing about the school, the classmates or the instructor (see section 2). Are the other students Northgladers, Arctics, or other runaways like her?
