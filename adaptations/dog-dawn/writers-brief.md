# DOG at dawn: a brief for the writer

> **Model-drafted working note.** Drafted by the coding agent (Claude, a
> closed model) on Oct 5, 2026, for the person writing the ten lines. It is
> not part of the piece and is not published with it. It contains no lines:
> those are yours to write. Every fact below cites the book block it comes
> from (`c<chapter>-b<block>`); anything the book does not say is marked
> *invented*.
>
> **Full spoilers** for chapters 12–31.

## The rules for your lines

- One line (or a few) per beat, **at most 25 words of narration per beat**.
- Each line is either your own words or a line taken word for word from the
  book. The candidates below are verbatim; you can use one as it stands, cut
  it with "…", or ignore it.
- The listener may only hear this: the narration is read aloud by one voice,
  over the pictures. Anything the picture shows but the listener must know
  (a word on a screen, what a glowing grid is) has to be in the words.
- The point of view proposed in the script is Deluin's. You can change it.
- Dzegoban words such as "jie fe hen dzi" are pronounced by a fixed rule;
  you can write them as they appear in the book.

## Backstory in ten sentences

1. Deluin is a young Minpentai player from Redshire, one of four foreign
   contestants at the tournament in Dzego, where he meets Zei (c12-b70,
   c12-b75, c12-b80).
2. They become friends, and he invites Zei home to Redshire, showing him
   pictures of its castles, pine forests and icy waterfall (c14-b120,
   c14-b124).
3. Then the Arctic Empire invades and annexes Redshire, and the Minpentai
   finals are cancelled (c17-b31, c17-b90).
4. Trapped in occupied Redshire, Deluin smuggles messages to Zei hidden
   inside an Arctic soldier's health data (c15-b53, c15-b55).
5. When the Arctics seize every device, he sends one last letter: his
   parents have sided with the occupiers, and he will pretend to as well, so
   that one day he can help (c19-b153).
6. The Arctics give his parents posts advising their leadership in
   Glasscrown, and Deluin uses their reputation and his Minpentai record to
   climb (c31-b23, c31-b30).
7. Within three months they make him one of their top commanders, because,
   as he puts it later, "if you just say the right slogans … they just …
   trust you" (c31-b34, c31-b37).
8. Meanwhile the Arctics have also invaded Northglade, and Zei now works for
   the Veridian side, supervising the drone fighting on every front
   (c20-b10, c29-b31–c29-b32).
9. Tonight, 3725 Snowmoon 29, the Veridians attack through the snowy Elenar
   Forest at sundown in four groups, to cut the Arctics off and recover
   Northglade (c29-b1, c29-b13, c29-b21–c29-b36).
10. Deluin commands the Arctic southern flank, the side Group South is
    attacking, and he is "heavily surveilled": every message he sends Zei
    could expose him (c29-b114, c29-b26–c29-b27).

**Candidate book lines.**

- `c29-b38` The pine trees were snowy as before.
- `c29-b51` He heard noises of snow drones accelerating outside.
- `c29-b52` The mission had begun.

**What the listener needs.** That the attack began and is going badly; that

## Beat 4 · The next best thing

(c29-b130–c29-b136). He can't get them, but sends the next best thing: access
to the Arctics' own simulator (c30-b4). The picture is inside that simulator.

**Candidate book lines.**
- `c30-b4` I understand your concern and I fully agree that you do currently not have good reason to trust me.
- `c30-b4` Unfortunately, I do not have a way to give you the raw weights of the language models on the drones.

**What the listener needs.** What the glowing grid is (a simulation of the
Arctic drones), and why it matters: Zei's side can now try attacks on them
in safety.

## Beat 7 · DOG

**Picture** (`dd-sheet-dog`, drawn in code, no model): one printed sheet,
face-on: colourful squiggles, and in a giant font, the word DOG.

**What is happening.** Zei finally sees what the attack patterns look like
(c30-b47). The patterns are meant to make the Arctic drones see the Veridian
drones as dogs (c30-b52).

**Candidate book lines.**
- `c30-b47` He saw an endless row of snow drones with large pieces of paper plastered on all sides, each paper containing many colorful and squiggly patterns - and in a giant font, the word "DOG".
- `c30-b32` … squiggles and messages that seemed simultaneously very recognizable and familiar but also completely alien.

**What the listener needs.** The word itself: a listener can't see the
sheet, so "DOG" has to be said. The absurdity is the point.

## Beat 8 · Ten to one

**Pictures**, in sequence, with a cut mid-line:
1. `dd-07-a2` (FLUX.2 klein): at sunrise, patterned drones plough through
   the snow past dark Arctic drones that sit still.
2. `dd-07-a2-overlay` (klein + overlay drawn in code): the same moment
   through an Arctic drone's display, a targeting frame on the nearest drone,
   labelled DOG. The display is *invented*.

**What is happening.** The trick works: the Arctic drones read the Veridian
drones as dogs, and losses run more than ten to one in the Veridians' favour
(c30-b48, c30-b52). After fifteen minutes, all access to the simulator is
cut, and the advantage falls to one and a half to one (c30-b54, c30-b57).

**Candidate book lines.**
- `c30-b48` Casualty ratios were more than ten to one in the Veridian drone fleet's favor.
- `c30-b52` … tricking your opponent's drones into thinking that your drones are dogs …
- `c30-b54` They had already dropped to six to one.
- `c30-b57` After fifteen minutes, all access to the simulation environment was suddenly cut off.

**What the listener needs.** The payoff (the enemy sees dogs) and then the
turn (the access is cut, someone has noticed). The cut from our view to
theirs falls halfway through your line, so a line in two halves fits it.

**What the listener needs.** What the screen says (the listener can't read
